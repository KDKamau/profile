Research Profile

Ken Dennis Kinyua is a PhD candidate in Information Systems at United States International University–Africa (USIU-A), Nairobi. His research sits at the intersection of distributed systems, IoT, and applied AI, with a focus on delivering technology-driven solutions for real-world challenges in East African and developing-economy contexts. He collaborates closely with faculty in the School of Science and Technology, with work spanning system observability for SMEs, road safety intelligence, and data-driven decision support — consistently emphasising affordability, accessibility, and local relevance.

Conference / Journal Paper
A Distributed Tracing Prototype for Nairobi SMEs: Addressing System Monitoring Challenges with AI-Enhanced Observability
Ken Dennis Kinyua & Alice Jeremoki
Department of Information Systems and Technology, United States International University–Africa
A lightweight distributed tracing prototype designed for Nairobi SMEs operating cloud-native and microservices environments. Built on OpenTelemetry, Jaeger, Prometheus, and Grafana, the system delivers automated instrumentation, centralised trace collection, real-time metrics, and accessible dashboards. Evaluation across 20 SMEs showed 85% cited cost as the foremost barrier to monitoring adoption. The prototype achieved an 83% improvement in error detection speed, 78% faster root cause analysis, and an 85% reduction in mean time to resolution, with trace completion rising from 43% to 94%.
Error detection: 83% faster Root cause analysis: 78% faster MTTR reduction: 85% Trace completion: 43% → 94%
Conference Paper
Data-Driven IoT Device for Proactive Road Safety: Mitigating Behavioural Risks in Nairobi County Roads
Ken Dennis Kinyua & Alice Jeremoki
School of Science and Technology, United States International University–Africa
Proposes a data-driven IoT decision-support system integrating LiDAR, ultrasonic sensors, radar, cameras, GPS, and accelerometers to generate real-time situational awareness and authorise overtaking only under verified safe conditions. Drawing on ADAS evidence showing 20–40% collision reductions, the framework employs edge computing and sensor fusion with system latencies under 50 ms. Survey data from 248 stakeholders confirmed a statistically significant positive influence of IoT integration on road safety management outcomes in Nairobi County.
Target collision reduction: 20–40% System latency: <50 ms Sample size: 248 respondents
